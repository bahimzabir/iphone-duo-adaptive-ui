# iPhone Duo Group Labs — Apple panel Q&A (Meet with Apple 285 / 286 / 287)

Source IDs: **LAB-1**, **LAB-2**, **LAB-3** (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/285/, /286/, /287/). Live Q&A sessions: Days 1 and 2 in mid-September 2026, a week after the Tech Talks and before Xcode 27.1 shipped; Day 3 about ten days before launch (mid-October 2026), with Xcode 27.1 and Device Hub already in developers' hands. Text below is from the English subtitle tracks, fetched **2026-10-26**, lightly trimmed of speech disfluencies; wording is otherwise the panel's. Speakers are Apple engineers and designers (UI frameworks, Human Interface design, TV app, Maps). 

**How to use this file.** These are spoken answers, not documentation. Where a statement is hedged on stage ("I believe", "I'm not sure") it is marked *hedged* and must not be presented as fact. Where a later written source covers the same point, prefer the written source (`apple-get-ready-prepare.md`, `apple-preparing-your-app-for-iphone-duo.md`, `hig-designing-for-iphone-duo.md`).

## Day 1 (LAB-1) — panel: UI frameworks engineer, two HI designers, TV app engineer, Maps engineer

**Can non-camera apps display things on the outer screen while unfolded?**
"There's a system entitlement that you can adopt in your app... the companion display for the camera is only available when there's an active camera session. So it's not enough just to have the entitlement. You actually have to be running a camera session to light up the inner and outer displays together." Later restated: "only camera apps... can use both displays at the same time, and only apps with an active camera session." Example given: Kid Cam shows "Snoopy... on the outer display to get the kids attention." → `hinge-multitasking-scenes.md` (scene accessories).

**Treat iPhone Duo as another adaptive size-class environment, or build Duo-specific layouts?**
"If you're using built-in components, they work really well and adapt for you... iPhone Duo is just another part of that seamless experience of layout." (frameworks) — "Every app should really be adapting to any size and expecting any size... then if there's any particular conventions for the device you're running on... you start to layer those on too. And for Duo, that's where the vertical controls start to come in." (design) — Ergonomics: "you're using this phone out of your pocket closed quite a lot. And so all your muscle memory starts to develop around those vertical controls. So when you open it, a lot of the time, you're going to reach for that same position on the screen." — "we certainly tried to make it feel very consistent between the closed pose and the open landscape pose... when you rotate into portrait... it's more of a two-handed mode... so we've changed the layout there."

**Seated pose with the camera side face down — can an app detect which half is on the table?** *hedged.* "I believe it does lay out like you can place the device like that and the interface works and it'll look the same as it does when it's flipped 180. Whether it's possible to detect that... I'm not totally sure." Design takeaway stated without hedge: "Expect people to use this device in and through all of the different ways of holding it... you really need to be thinking about supporting all of the different ways that might be used."

**Will a custom tab bar at the bottom automatically adapt?**
"You are creating a `UITabBar` and inserting that as a subview. It won't automatically be placed in the vertical bar. If you're using a tab bar controller or a `TabView` in SwiftUI, then that functionality you'll get automatically." — "there are reserved regions and ways to do that alignment, but there are a lot of places that vertical bars can appear... When the device is closed, as you rotate it, the vertical bar always aligns with where the camera is, so it can be on the left. And if you're in Split View multitasking... the left-hand app will have a left-hand bar. And so there's just a lot of asymmetry that you need to take care of." — Maps/Find My: "this year we did a lot of work to migrate all our screens over to navigation controller and tab bar controller in order to get the vertical bar." — System tab bar behavior to match if you insist on custom: "we show the labels on touchdown... when you touch down and start dragging, it's clear what all the tabs are."

**One thing every developer should adopt?** Vertical bar (Maps); `ArrangementView` — "TV is using AVKit, which uses it to move the video to the top and controls to the bottom when you position the phone like a laptop" (TV); designing for each pose (design). Frameworks engineer on choosing the API layer: "From the lowest level... you can get the actual angle that you folded to. And then... we'll give you basically a rect on the screen when you've properly folded the device to a certain angle [reserved regions]. And then on top of that, for developers who might be using lower level layout primitives like an `HStack` or a `ZStack`, we came up with this `ArrangementView` concept... And then... all of the main system components from alerts and sheets and split view controllers, all of those things adapt... figure out for the experience that you're trying to build, which is the proper API... it's fun to... say, I want the pure angle of the hinge and do some wild math... but the reserved region API might be a better fit for that, or an `ArrangementView`."

**Minimizing letterboxing/pillarboxing on launch day; what each SDK gives you.**
"if you're linking against Xcode 26 on the inner display, you will be letterboxed. Your app will appear in iPhone regular on other iPhone aspect ratio with black bars on the sides. Once you build with Xcode 27... you support resizability. You'll use almost the full interior screen, but there'll still be a black bar on the side underneath the status bar. And... you build with Xcode 27.1. Then you'll go to full screen." On the outer display with a pre-27 SDK: "you will see basically the ratio of an iPhone mini for your application. So there will be a letterbox on the side where the vertical bar would be. When you open up... that same ratio will be in the middle of the screen... in both landscape and portrait... you don't get any reactivity to any of the different poses." Testing tip: "resizing your app on iPhone Mirroring. You're going to be able to get an aspect ratio that's pretty close to that... because that experience also doesn't change... the type of device that the system thinks you're on." Written version: `apple-get-ready-prepare.md` §2 "Before you begin".

**Will Xcode ship an agentic skill for Duo?** "Xcode 27.1... is going to have an updated version of the modernize your app skill that's updated to support SwiftUI in addition to UIKit. And that knows about iPhone Duo." Maps used it: "we had used a little bit of it in Maps actually for... `UIScreen.main`, and it worked really well." → shipped; see `apple-app-resizability-skill.md`.

**App locked to portrait — does it still get size-class changes when folding?**
"on the outer display, we will honor supported orientations, but on the inner display, we won't. Yeah, and so it will adapt. And the size class will be regular, regular on the inside." — "There's things like `UIRequiresFullScreen` and we strongly recommend that if you can get off of those APIs... There are many different types of APIs, especially in UIKit around orientation and some of those may not reflect what you may naturally think on this new display. And so we really want you to go off of the size classes." Rationale: "As soon as you go from a portrait aspect ratio to square, now it's a little bit wider. Like do you not support landscape? Where do you draw the line?" *[note: this is the clearest Apple statement reconciling the two TT-PREPARE passages quoted in `device-facts.md` → "Orientation behavior".]*

**Accessibility nuances.** Dynamic Type at the largest sizes "are going to look really good when they open their phone"; use "the readable content guide"; "it's one of the first times where VoiceOver is working on two displays at once." **Reduce Transparency:** "when you turn that on, it will basically draw an opaque rectangle behind the vertical bar. And it's a little bit narrower than the safe area for the vertical bar because you didn't want it to directly touch content. And so... turning that on and making sure your app looks good with it on."

**One canvas or two displays?** "we want it to be as cohesive as possible because some tasks will be started closed and finished open... one singular thing, not a phone when it's closed and iPad when it's open... you don't have to manage windows differently between the two states or have different settings for the different states." — "when the phone is closed and you open it, you're just resizing your app to be wider... your whole app is a resizable window that you're dragging, except you're just unfolding this phone."

**First-party examples.** Maps: "when we're portrait closed, we have the sheet coming from the bottom. When you open it, the sheet gets leading placement. So you see a lot more map on the right side. So it's using the iPad layout on the inner display... similar to closed landscape." Maps also reframes the map "to make sure that, say, if you're viewing a place card, it's centered in the screen." TV: "when we open, we basically show more content... in the homepage we have this hero header where we show trailers. So when we open, we can add the description of the trailer and some more info." Find My: "the tab bar was already embedded in the sheet. So we just kept that... if the tabs apply to all of the states of the sheet, then it makes sense for them to be together... But there might be a different app where a tab has its own sheet. And maybe they do make sense to be separated."

**Three-column iPad layout on Duo?** "you get two columns at a time... there's a button in the top left that shows you the root column as an overlay... if you're in portrait... looking at the Mail app, you'll see just the detail view. And if you press the button in the top left, you'll get the sidebar as an overlay and you can pull it in and actually see those two root views next to each other." Same as "a Pro Max in landscape." Components: `NavigationSplitView` / `UISplitViewController`; `TabView` / `UITabBarController` "can show the tabs as a sidebar when you're in open wide." Bonus: "when you go partially open, like a book, then the split view automatically resizes and takes up 50/50 of the screen."

**Safari/WebKit APIs for poses?** *hedged, no WebKit expert present.* "there's some existing JavaScript standards around foldable devices. I would look to those... I'm not an expert on WebKit... try it out and file feedback." → Apple still publishes no Duo web guidance; `device-facts.md` "NOT documented" stands.

**Does each pose need manual adoption?** "if you follow the best practices, the app will look great. I think you could go a step further and really think about how to optimize your app for the specific pose. If there's anything contextual to what the app is doing... the video player and the podcast player, the controls are at the bottom when it's seated on a table." Design lesson: "we kind of had this overlay of all the different poses... it almost created a goal out of filling each pose with something exciting... as we got further along, we realized there's some staples that if you support those, you get support across everything... don't force it... don't feel stressed about having to sizzle up every single pose." Transitions: "if you have one pose UI in seated pose and it's entirely different than when you're flat, you have to think about how that actually transitions. Can be disorienting." TV example: controls "slide apart. It's like your eye follows to where the pieces are going... if you use `ArrangementView`s, you will just get that."

**Inner display: distinguish landscape vs portrait when both are regular/regular?** Design: "you start getting into available width. So you might go to a two column... in the App Store... we might show two columns of things landscape and then a single column of things vertical... but it should still feel like the same content, just rearranged." Technical: "although we don't respect the supported orientations on the inner display, we do provide the current orientation in the environment and as a trait. So between the size classes and reading the orientation, technically you can differentiate." Immediately qualified: "I do think there are better ways... You can make a very thin window on iPad that is technically in landscape orientation... really thinking about what is the width you have and the content that you're trying to display... if you have these container level framework components, they're going to do the heavy lifting for you." *[note: consistent with DUO-PREPARE "compare the width and height of the space you have".]*

**Can I keep the regular iPhone behavior on Xcode 27 while I finish months of Duo work?** "There is not. Once you link on iOS 27, you get iPhone resizability." — "you don't have to on day one have the absolute perfect pose layout for every pose... app teams, even with just 1 or 2 engineers, were able to pretty quickly get something that looks great."

**What resizable-simulator / iPhone Mirroring testing catches vs. what needs the Duo simulator.** "with resizability you can detect things... if you interact with the corner of the window... you're not following the correct layout guides... On the Duo, you would find more things like the vertical bar, which makes the safe area margins asymmetric... most of the issues you would find with just iPhone Mirroring." — "a lot of phone apps that have been locked to portrait have only worried about top and bottom safe areas... the unsafe areas are on the side often now." — "the content insets are also asymmetrical... use the real content insets from the system and not assume that one side is equal to the other side."

**Full-screen-only app (e.g. immersive AR)?** "it would be the equivalent of just building an app like any other app and hiding the status bar. And you've got a full screen app. You don't have to use the vertical controls if it doesn't make sense."

**Biggest design mistake seen?** "trying to design for every pose and pulling back from that."

**Multiple instances of the same app?** "It works in a very similar way to iPad... you can have multiple views of your app open at the same time... side by side with each other or... paired with other apps." Shared state: "if both instances are using, for example, app storage, do they share the same state? Yes, the same app... the same update mechanisms that SwiftUI or UIKit currently use."

**Core Motion reference frame on Duo?** *not answered* ("it's magic, you don't need to worry about it").

**Also stated in the opening:** "when iPhone Duo becomes available on October 23rd, it will run iOS 27.1"; "the Figma and Sketch design kits are available from the Apple Developer website" *[note: the Design Resources page fetched 2026-10-26 lists an iPhone Duo entry under Photoshop/PNG; no separate Figma or Sketch Duo file is named on that page. Check developer.apple.com/design/resources/ before telling a user which formats exist.]*

## Day 2 (LAB-2) — panel: UI frameworks engineer (bars), Music/Podcasts/iTunes Store engineer, Phone/FaceTime engineer, HI design-systems designer

**App full-screen on the inner display, user closes the device — does it move or get backgrounded?**
"It'll move to the outer display... you close the device and it's just like the app has gotten smaller." Split View edge case (*relayed, "my understanding from" colleagues*): "The system will try to promote whichever one you are interacting with to the outer display. And the other one will be backgrounded... If you then open iPhone Duo again, if it was rather recently that you closed it, they'll both come back. But if it's been a while... the app they were using on the outer display [takes] the whole inner display." Timing not shared.

**Simulating before the Duo simulator existed.** "run your app on iPad and just turn on window resizing... make sure you follow best practices for window scenes usage... don't use the main screen... do it through the window scene." Plus iPhone Mirroring for iPhone-only apps. (Superseded by DUO-PREPARE Step 3.)

**Touch input from both displays at once in the L pose?** "I think no." (*hedged*; the only two-display case is the camera accessory.)

**Tab items with text labels only, no icons?** "if you're only using text labels and you haven't set an icon that will still be able to go vertical. However... by default the tab bar shows the icon representation, and when someone interacts with it, that's when you'll start seeing the labels. So... it might be a little bit confusing." Design: "making those icons happen... We have a very big library of SF Symbols... over 7,400." Behavior to know: "when a person is scrubbing the tab icons in the bar on the side, the labels do appear."

**Custom bars.** "first consider if you can use the system one... You do not want to get into the business of building your own. But if there's some reason that you absolutely can't. One API we have that is new in 27.1 is the ability to grab out the region that the system bar would draw in... you can request, hey, I want a bar that is, you know, like the trailing bar, and then you can know where you should put it." Music and Podcasts use that region: "music specifically to place the lyrics, the voice attenuation button... and the translation button." *[note: the API was not named on stage; the closest documented surfaces are `toolbarVerticalEdge` / `verticalBarEdge` and reserved regions — see `api-index.md`; do not present an unnamed API as a specific symbol.]* Later: "even if you're using `UITabBar`, you're also not going to get the adaptation to the vertical axis... if you're using `UITabBar`, `UIToolbar`, `UINavigationBar`, that is just going to stay horizontal. It will never go vertical... you need to use the containers." And: "we generally have APIs to place things in the correct axis, but we don't necessarily have APIs to make them behave the same... showing the labels on tap... lensing when you're scrubbing... There's also a lot of complexity in the compression logic."

**Duo landscape vs iPad landscape — how much should the UI differ?** "space availability first... start with size classes, design for that. And then what differs will be platform standards between iPad and iPhone." — "a lot of people's natural reaction was to pull those iPad designs. And we had to do a lot of work to... be like, okay, this is now the iPad design, but this is a phone... let's make sure it feels like a phone... potentially strip out anything or change it, guard it, maybe behind an idiom if it feels like it should belong only on iPad." Phone app example: "on the iPad phone app, we have the dialer in a popover... this is a phone. And so we... promoted the dialer to its own tab." Music: "the sidebar is probably the biggest difference, but we weren't making any assumption... is this an iPad or is this an iPhone."

**Inner display in tent/standing pose for non-camera apps (alarm-clock glow)?** "the answer is no... two displays on at the same time... is generally the camera accessory, which is really only for camera apps." The alarm glow "is provided by AlarmKit." Tent pose in general: "for most cases, just like your closed landscape view, outer display landscape view, just kind of propped up on a table for glanceability... it would just be your standard layout."

**Duo-only app?** *hedged* ("I have no idea") — design answer: "why would you want your app to run only on this device... you just support the whole lineup." Designing for one pose only: "no, probably not. People are going to use the device as they want to."

**iPad-only apps in a compatibility mode?** *hedged* — guidance: "make the app universal and [add] iPhone to the list of supported platforms."

**Preserving navigation/UI state when switching displays.** "if you're using system navigation controllers, they will adapt for you. It's not like your app is getting created on one display and then torn down and put on another display. It's really that you're getting resized." Music's one Duo-specific behavior: "if you open the device, we put you into the lyrics mode because of course we have more space... If you close it, we put you back to normal." SwiftUI state-loss warning: "in a compact size class, they will use one container on one side of an if statement, and then they'll use a different container on the other side... guarded by size class. So if you were doing that, you will see state loss. If you haven't raised everything up... Consider this as an opportunity to see if you actually need to be doing that."

**One thing to do before the Duo simulator.** Resizing on iPad / iPhone Mirroring; "asymmetric safe areas and content margins... if you've got an app that has been locked in portrait mode, you might only be checking top and bottom safe areas... test your app in landscape." — "people will check one side and say, oh, the other side must be the same... Look for a two x on a width. `.left` times two. Do not."

**Camera apps with fake auto-rotation (portrait-locked, rotating elements individually).** Two APIs: the rotation coordinator ("`AVCaptureDevice` rotation coordinator... will help you figure out the right rotation to do for your camera view") and the direction API ("Since there's two front cams, you may have previously made assumptions that the front cam is the one facing you. But that's not always true... that API will tell you... what direction the camera you're using is facing"). → TT-CAMERA; `hinge-multitasking-scenes.md`.

**How do I know the pose (laptop/desktop)? Do I need hinge APIs? How does `AVPlayerViewController` do it?** "system containers will adapt... my first question would be, what are you trying to do there? Because the guidance is you want your app to feel similar across poses. It's adapting, but it's not massively changing... one of the new APIs that I would think about is `ArrangementView`s, where it's a little bit more semantic." Music: "I've been using the reserved region to know where the division is... arrange your layout whenever it is active or not." Design: "Your layout probably in 90% of the cases, does not need to do anything special in those poses... I wouldn't overoptimize straight out the gate... when it's partially folded, is this going to be a dominant way people use this phone? Or... a transient moment... We don't know. We're all going to learn together."

**Hardest first-party app to adapt?** iTunes Store: "it took me probably just a week to move to window scenes... and to adopt Liquid Glass... just those two actually helped a lot with iPhone Duo experience by itself. We didn't do any pose specific stuff." FaceTime: "direct them towards looking at that [inner] camera because it is kind of in a unique location on the right side... we tried to put the most gravity in the UI towards that area"; the self-view PiP dodges the inner camera via the reserved-region API — "that can come and go... when the camera turns off, it's not there anymore."

**Scene accessories on the outer display — animation/update-rate limits?** "You get a full UI scene... It's not like widgets where you have to be careful about what content you can put in them." Control: "you do have to have an active camera session in order to turn on the scene accessory. But when the camera is on, you can freely control that... add a button in your UI that toggles the accessory off and on... observe when the accessory is available... if you're in Split View, when the iPhone Duo is open, then you also can't use scene accessories."

**Audio mixing with two instances in Split View?** *hedged* — "two scenes of a single app... I would expect it to not be possible to control them separately... try it on iPad."

**Keeping content away from the hinge without hard-coding dimensions.** "Please don't hard code layout specific dimensions... alerts is a great example... We've done a lot of that for you. However, if you do have components that sit in the fold that are in the content area... a `UIButton`... revenue driving actions... buy now... add to cart... start free trial... Those things do need to move. So those are perfect ones to use reserved regions. If you ever want to move just a single element, that's the perfect API."

**External monitor / Stage Manager?** External display: "it does work" (*relayed*). Stage Manager: "I think the answer there is going to be no."

**Asymmetric insets in Split View on the hinge side; SwiftUI layout guides.** "when it moves to the left... there will be a left safe area, but then you won't see it on the right." SwiftUI "there is a new API for that... a modifier that you can put on... that'll apply the margins for you" (*name not recalled on stage; unverified*). UIKit: "there are layout guides that you can attach to. And so just make sure you're doing that on all four edges." For apps without bars that "really just want to know if there's a camera there or if they need to respect the status bar. You can look at the corner adaptation and so then you can just respect that specific region and then you can draw everywhere else... if you're a full screen game app." Outer-display corners "don't have the same radius... the ones away from the hinge are rounder"; "it's also the only place that we have a bidirectional status bar" (horizontal in inner-portrait, vertical in the side bar).

**Testing hinge poses in the simulator.** "you can change the hinge angle, and then you can also put it into the different poses... book, open, closed, laptop, tent... take a look at the transitions between poses." New in 27.1: "the simulator will launch apps that use the camera. It won't show anything in the camera view. It will detect that there are no cameras available."

**Sheets with controls (Find My-style).** "whether your sheet has or does not have controls laid out to the side, the controls kind of stay with the sheet... If you have it, you'll probably continue to have it. If you don't, you'll probably continue having it horizontal." Writing tools / Notes sheets: "a vertical bar kind of felt distracting." Find My observed live: portrait outer → "tab bar on the side and the sheet half open"; rotate → "tab bar at the bottom and the sheet on the leading side. So you don't want to cover the map." — "Find My has chosen to opt in to a different width. And that kind of comes with different choices on your bars."

**Compression and overflow.** "The height of the vertical bar on the edge can change so much. Rotating the outer display, showing a video PiP above the display, moving to Split View multitasking." — "we've also never had bars sitting together... now everything's pretty much on top of each other... even on a regular height, will just now start compressing." — "if your app maybe hasn't had to handle compression or overflow menus before... How does it behave when items move into the overflow? ... set priorities so that the system can preserve the things that are most important for the longest amount of time."

**Does iPhone Duo have its own idiom?** "It is a phone... it responds to a phone idiom... It does have its own unique combination of size classes on the inner display... regular, regular, which is the first time a phone has had that." — "if you had something that's like iPad idiom, so I'm going to show the iPad UI versus phone idiom, so I'm never going to show the iPad UI... start looking at size classes now."

**Widgets on the lock screens?** *hedged* — "we have the new extra large ones on the inner display. I do not believe that those can exist on the outer display... They're six by four." StandBy works off-charger on this device.

**New APIs for foldable layouts; do they work on other phones?** `ArrangementView`: "almost every single [first-party] app has an ArrangementView." — "It does actually work on our other hardware as well. As long as the conditions that you've set up the ArrangementView are true on the device that it's running on... if you still have space for two columns, it will render as two columns. If you don't, it will move towards a single view, which is exactly what happens when you move between inner and outer display." — "good functionality should not be behind idioms."

**Speed round.** Portrait vs landscape on the outer display: "why do you need to know that?... On the outer display, the size classes are different in the two axes." Hinge angle: "with the hinge interaction you can get the angle" (unit not recalled; UIKit DocC says radians, SwiftUI uses `Angle`). Web: "You can't specifically check whether it's a Duo... there is standard web API for checking whether a device has a fold and is folded." Back-deployment: "an availability check... make the stuff that is Duo specific... have an iOS 27 availability check"; for `ArrangementView` "maintain using that existing layout... when ArrangementViews are available on those operating systems, switch." Multi-window: "It does. We call it Split View multitasking."

## Day 3 (LAB-3, ten days before launch) — panel: two HI designers (system; Messages/Files/Measure), two UI frameworks engineers (tab bars/menus; bars/navigation)

**Designing one app across poses.** "you're making one app for the iPhone, whether it's an iPhone Duo or the iPhone Pro... the same type of controls... the same kind of hierarchy, the same kind of navigation... continuous as you move between it. So people know where to look for controls." — "within a single phone, your app's going to get used in a lot more states than you might for just other phones."

**Which side should a custom view move to when avoiding the fold?** "we tend to avoid more to the right because we found that more people are more likely to hold it that way... But... other elements that are maybe smaller... you would almost want to avoid it towards the side that it is mostly on... context menus and the edit menu for text editing... will tend to stay on the side that the source view is on." — hierarchy drives it: "primary on the left and the detail on the right... things are already placed in an appropriate position, and then they can just shift over." Seated pose: "keep the interactive controls closer to the bottom... a stable surface to press against." Closing the device: "if you have a primary and a detail layout, oftentimes that detail will be the thing that you see when the device closes... because the detail is the furthest down the hierarchy."

**Custom two-pane split with `GeometryReader` keeping a stale width after rotation.** "there's `ArrangementView`s, which you can style to be a split arrangement and... you can customize that ratio of the columns. And in a lot of cases that can eliminate things that you might have previously been using a `GeometryReader` for... it also will work on other iPhones." Stale sizes from `GeometryReader` on rotation: "worth filing Feedback."

**Do we need physical hardware?** "No, you don't need a physical iPhone Duo to test... there is a great Simulator experience that you can use to end-to-end test your app in all of the different states and poses." Exceptions: camera streaming, hardware-specific APIs, and feel ("You might make different decisions on a physical device"). "the Simulator that's in the betas right now doesn't have most of the first-party apps in it."

**StandBy, widgets, Live Activities.** "all the different sizes of widgets that we support in StandBy for other phones directly translate to the Duo. We've introduced a new... extra large... basically fills like half of the Duo screen when it's open... not just for the Duo, but for other devices too." Book pose: "time and date and calendar on one side and then a Live Activity open on the other side."

**Camera apps: is opening the device a camera-direction change or a layout transition?** "It's not an either or. It's a both." Rear camera: "keep the cameras where they were... the customer probably just wants more screen real estate." Front camera (video calling): "the switch from the closed display camera to the internal camera... should happen automatically if you're using the right APIs... you really need to be using `AVCaptureDevice` RotationCoordinator and DirectionCoordinator." Aspect choice: "when the iPhone Duo is open, the camera on the back is still a portrait aspect ratio, right? But the screen is in a landscape aspect ratio... do you want to show a portrait preview inside of the landscape screen with letterboxing, or do you want to zoom it up and fill the screen?... in the Camera app, the default is the camera preview is center frame... in AR... ARKit, we went edge to edge and zoomed in."

**Seated pose stability and control placement.** "it's sturdier than you would expect"; still, "placing controls where you may be really using them frequently on the bottom can not only be good from a device position perspective... but also just from a reachability standpoint." Three things to weigh: "If there is primary content, like a video, you probably want that included. So shifting the controls to the bottom makes a lot of sense. There are benefits just from interacting on the tabletop surface... But then the third thing to consider is we try to avoid having controls move significantly when you go between poses... start with as little as possible, unless there's enough value that it's worth it in that new pose." — "trust your users because I think they'll gravitate towards the best way to use your app."

**Top three fixes for an existing iPhone-only app (panel's combined list).**
1. "adjusting for the asymmetric safe area and making sure that you don't have content that's going under horizontal safe areas or hard to reach."
2. "making sure that you have the right controls showing up in the vertical layout of the toolbars and navigation bars... that you don't have controls that are showing up without icons."
3. "readable content margins... whether you're just adding additional margin in those landscape poses so things aren't too wide to read or you're finding additional columns to show." — "lines of text being across the full width of the screen will look a little bit wonky... We have this concept of readable content width."
Also: single `NavigationStack` apps → "maybe switching to more of a `NavigationSplitView` so that you can show more of your hierarchy, like Mail"; horizontal carousels sized from "the device width, subtracting maybe some padding" → "capping to some aspect ratio... Instead of just enlarging the content, you now just surface more content"; portrait-locked apps carry hidden assumptions; "`UINavigationController` also supports toolbar items at the bottom, and those are the ones you want to use" instead of a positioned `UIToolbar`; bars on Duo "share the space with the status bar in both portrait and landscape when it's open... And when there's a Live Activity coming and going, how are you going to resize and move out of the way"; "an overflow menu inside their overflow menu" → use the system overflow APIs; custom toolbar views "the system isn't able to move that to be vertical automatically, because it doesn't know how wide it needs to be... if you're using a custom view for something that's effectively an image... just set the image on the item"; `axisBehavior` "prefers vertical... doesn't mean it will go vertical on other phones. It will still stay horizontal... you don't need to do anything that's conditional."

**CI / UI tests: no `simctl` or `UIDevice` hooks for hinge state.** "We can't talk to future plans... file Feedback so that they get exposed" with the use case (snapshot testing, transitions). iPhone resizing on a Mac remains useful.

**WebKit.** *relayed*: "My understanding is that the web standards include support for hinge position and that WebKit supports that."

**Games in the half-folded pose (gameplay top, controls bottom).** "You absolutely can... the best thing to use for that would probably be an `ArrangementView`. If you use the overlay mode... it can automatically stack the items on top of each other when you're not in that kind of pose. But then when you are in that kind of pose, they can show side by side with the controls on the bottom and the content on the top. Very similar to... AVKit video players... you can read if they're stacked on top of each other or not" (`overlayArrangementZIndex`). Precise avoidance for custom bars: "reading the corner adaptation... I have like this vertical safe area on the whole side, but actually I really only want to... avoid it a little bit more precisely." — "also works on iPad, because the window controls on iPad also take up the space... And Mac Catalyst as well." (`UIView.LayoutRegion.safeArea(cornerAdaptation:)`, SDK-verified — `api-index.md`.) Games generally: "making sure your game supports any aspect ratio and display size... when you are in those poses, for example, it's placed on a surface almost like a laptop. That could be a great time to move your controls down into the bottom area."

**Preserving an unfinished form / scroll position when one column becomes two.** "using a navigation container or something like an `ArrangementView` that is going to preserve your state across the transition... there's nothing in doing that that is intrinsically going to tear your app down... However, that will change the size class. And... sometimes you may have a big if statement at the root of your app that is switching on: if compact, if regular, that is going to tear down all the state." Design: "when you're introducing that second column: are there any surprise moments... in Mail, we might choose to show the message that was open last. For another app... It might be surprising." — "sometimes you don't actually want to automatically pick one... opening it up and it would select an unread conversation, sending a read receipt, you probably don't want to do that... we do have a content unavailable view."

**Is the scene or window recreated on fold/unfold? Do `onAppear` / `onDisappear` / `scenePhase` fire?** *hedged on the details* — "I would expect `onAppear` and `onDisappear` not to get called again... your app would be moving to a different screen. However, your apps, your view controllers, your views, all those instances should remain the same... treating this as an adaptive layout resize... They're not different copies of your app on the other screen and the inner screen." One real `scenePhase` case: "If your app wasn't the one that was most recently interacted with [in Split View], it can go into the background when the device is closed." Also: "I would recommend not doing `UIScreen.main`. It will not work well" → the Xcode 27.1 modernization skill.

**Fully open and flat: still avoid the middle?** "It can go either way... apps where there is a natural two-column layout, it's preferable to have the two-column layout because then that partial fold transition isn't as drastic... `NavigationSplitView` or `UISplitViewController`... You'll often have a 35/65 layout. And then when you go into the folded state, then you'll have something more like 50/50... what you don't want is a very large layout transition when you go from the flattened to the folded state." Carousels "scroll right on through" the hinge. Scroll direction: "scrolling perpendicular to the hinge can work pretty well... when you're scrolling is parallel to the hinge, it can be a little bit more difficult... very difficult to reach in and actually perform a scroll right in that hinge area." Quick Look PDFs scroll horizontally in book pose. Technical trick: "request for the region ahead of time... requesting the reserved regions and asking for the inactive option" so a 50/50 layout needs no transition when folding (`.includeInactive`). App Store article in landscape (hero left, article right) avoids the fold; TV in portrait does not ("you would only have half a screen to scroll in") — "in open portrait, you might make a different choice than when it's open landscape."

**Tab bar vs sidebar on the inner display (TV uses a tab bar on Duo, sidebar on iPad).** "really depends on what you're prioritizing... apps more content-driven... navigating, looking through the content of the app versus navigating the different destinations." — "even the smaller-sized iPads still have larger displays than the iPhone Duo." — "The difference on the Duo is you're working with two sizes of the app... trying to be consistent between the closed and open states becomes a factor... the apps that I feel work pretty well for the sidebar tend to be ones where in the sidebar they're representing a view that would be available when it's closed. So you would see that in like Settings." — "I launched TV not because I want to think right now."

**Long `UITextView` reflow on unfold — is reading position kept?** "it does actually already baked into `UITextView` and various other `ScrollView`-based components. We have `UICollectionView` stable views... as your view resizes, we do actually anchor it to the visible position." Inner display regular/regular: "Yes."

**Navigation bar large title and bar items scrolling away.** "on iPhone Duo, by default, the bars do actually scroll away when, I believe, you're closed and also maybe in landscape, but there is an API on `UINavigationItem` as well as toolbar behaviors... in SwiftUI, that will allow you to change that behavior... this API isn't just exclusive to the Duo... you can force it on other phones or you can force it off." *[note: API not named on stage; unverified here.]* Design: "depending on the orientation of your `ScrollView`, you may want to flip the direction that the navigation bar scrolls off... in Messages it scrolls off when you're scrolling back in the history." Rationale: "iPhone Duo has a shorter display than other iPhones. And so it can be really valuable to maximize that content area."

**Picture in Picture.** "works the same way as other iPhones... The new feature... if you're in Portrait Open, there's a new control that lets you pin the video to the top of the screen... you can launch another app and use it at the bottom of the screen, and the video won't occlude the app."

**Minimum work for a custom floating tab bar.** "two APIs to check out are first, you have the ability to see what edge the vertical bar is laying out on [`verticalBarEdge` / `toolbarVerticalEdge`]... and you also want to take a look at the ability to get the bar region to see where the system would be drawing... take your horizontal bar and then move it into that vertical region based on whether or not there's a vertical bar." Alternative: "bar button items support having a custom view... you're okay with having the system glass and the system laying that out, that can be a way to reduce the workload." *[note: the "bar region" API is again unnamed on stage; see Day 2 note.]*
